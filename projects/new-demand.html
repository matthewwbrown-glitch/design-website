Upgrading the ads creation funnel for Shops sellers

I led the design of ‘New Demand’, an initiative to upgrade the ads creation funnel for sellers, from the first prompt in the Shopify Meta App to a 1-click ready-to-publish campaign in Ads Manager.

Overview

Sellers managing their shop in the Shopify Meta App and Commerce Manager had a sub-optimal way to start advertising. Getting from their shop to a running ad meant leaving their workspace, landing in Ads Manager and configuring a campaign from scratch.

As the lead product designer on the Shopify Meta App, I designed the end-to-end funnel across these seller surfaces, where sellers are guided through a campaign proposal that’s ready to publish.

The Challenge

The existing path to creating an ad lost sellers at every step.

  • Prompts didn’t meet sellers where they were. Ad prompts weren’t tied to what a seller was doing in their workspace, so few started the journey.
  • Creation was manual and multi-step. Sellers had to configure campaign settings themselves, from scratch.
  • Ads didn’t always run. Many ads that sellers did create couldn’t deliver because of setup and connection issues.
  • The funnel spanned several surfaces. The experience had to feel like one journey across the Shopify Meta App, Commerce Manager and Ads Manager, each owned by different partner teams.

Approach

I treated ads creation as a single funnel with three stages, and improved it over two rounds.

1. Entry: inviting sellers to advertise

We audited the seller journey to find the moments where advertising made sense, and placed prompts within sellers’ existing jobs to be done.

  • New upsells. I designed upsells for sellers with and without ad credits, so the offer matched each seller’s situation.
  • Entry points by seller state. In the second round, I created multiple entry points tailored to where each seller was in their journey.
  • A consistent prompt system. A new promo card component and mobile touchpoints, built on the ‘Guidance Platform’ framework, gave every prompt the same dismiss logic, impression caps and cool-downs.

2. Proposal: a ready-made campaign instead of manual setup

I replaced the manual, multi-step configuration with a ready-made campaign proposal showing only the details sellers needed. In the first round, sellers had to leave the Shopify Meta App and go to Ads Manager to see it. In the second round, I brought the proposal into the Shopify Meta App itself: sellers could review it in a modal without leaving their workspace, fix common issues inline and decide whether to proceed. The modal showed recommended settings alongside a preview of the ad, and flagged problems such as a pixel not connected to the ad account so sellers could fix them on the spot.

3. Hand-off: landing in Ads Manager ready to publish

Sellers who chose to proceed landed in Ads Manager with their campaign already filled in, ready to publish.

Implementation & Collaboration

  • Cross-team alignment. I worked with the AdEx team to agree on the seller cohorts we were targeting, so every surface spoke to the same sellers.
  • Technical partnership. I worked closely with partner teams, along with existing data and research, to determine which manual controls were kept and which steps we automated.
  • Systematic design. I maintained a single source of truth for the design library, so edge cases for each seller cohort were handled.

Results & Impact

New Demand tested one hypothesis over two rounds: if creating an ad takes fewer steps, more sellers will advertise.

Round 1: Testing the hypothesis

We tested whether a ready-made campaign proposal would get more sellers advertising. Sellers clicked a new upsell in the Shopify Meta App and were taken to Ads Manager, where a modal showed the proposal with only the details they needed. Even with that jump between apps, compared with the existing flow:

  • Click-through rate on the upsell rose 124%.
  • Conversion rate went from 5% to 24%.
  • Deliverable ad rate went from 30% to 53%. More of the ads sellers created were able to run.
  • 67% of advertisers kept spending. They maintained daily spend on Shops ads for three consecutive days.

The experiment didn’t ship, but it confirmed the hypothesis: fewer steps meant more sellers creating ads that ran. That signal funded a second round in H2, and the clearest step left to remove was the trip to Ads Manager just to see the proposal.

Round 2: Cutting steps even further

In round 2, sellers no longer had to leave the Shopify Meta App. From entry points tailored to their state, the proposal opened in a modal right where they were, with common issues fixed inline. Sellers could review it and decide whether to proceed, and only those ready to publish moved on to Ads Manager. The launched funnel improved conversion by 46% compared with H1.

Reflection

New Demand showed me the value of treating a no-ship as a step rather than an ending. The first round proved the hypothesis, and the data showed exactly where to invest: keeping sellers in the Shopify Meta App to review their proposal, and fixing issues before they reached Ads Manager. Designing the funnel as one journey, rather than as separate screens owned by separate teams, is what let the second round launch.
