Meta · Shopify Meta App · 2024

Streamlining Shops onboarding and global stakeholder alignment

As lead product designer for the Shopify Meta App, I led the strategy and design for Shops onboarding in Ads Manager, aligning cross-functional teams across global offices to deliver a unified seller experience.

Overview

As the lead product designer for the Shopify Meta App, I spearheaded the strategy and design for the Shops Onboarding experience within Ads Manager. This initiative was critical for expanding our Total Addressable Market (TAM) by enabling merchants to seamlessly adopt onsite checkout. My role focused on navigating constraints and bringing cross-functional teams across global offices together on one end-to-end seller experience.

The Challenge

Following a recent re-org, the project faced significant risks due to team misalignment and “blurred lines” regarding project ownership. Working across time zones meant late-night and early-morning calls, where misalignments were hard to resolve over Zoom.

  • Team misalignment, different pillars had conflicting priorities for how the “Orange” (medium-friction) and “Green” (low-friction) seller cohorts should be onboarded.
  • High ambiguity, I had to manage the design needs of several high-impact projects at once while adjusting to new working conditions and reduced content design support.
  • Technical complexity, the solution required updated flows across nine different surfaces and three outreach mechanisms (email, AYMT and Shopify Resource Feedback).

Strategic Approach: The London Design Sprint

To resolve the mounting misalignment, I planned and facilitated a 2-day in-person design workshop in London. The escalation brought cross-functional partners from across the globe into one room to finalise the product experience.

1. Facilitating stakeholder alignment

  • The room where it happened, I led the strategy and design sprint for the “Orange” cohort, while supporting the Shops Engagement product designers on the “Green” cohort.
  • Visualising the E2E journey, using whiteboards, FigJam and prototypes, I mapped the end-to-end onboarding experience for both cohorts, so every cross-functional partner, from engineering to product, was aligned.
  • Building a working model, once we were aligned, I established a working model that stabilised the team’s momentum and let us deliver milestone 1 and 2 designs on time.

2. Design and execution

After the workshop, I turned what we had aligned on in London into something the team could build from. I documented the decisions captured on the whiteboard, along with the open questions, and brought it together in a high-fidelity prototype in Figma, built from atomic components that scaled across every surface. Both teams could continue commenting async on the finer details, but the core experience was locked in. The quick turnaround kept the momentum from London and unblocked the team to start building.

Results & Impact

The London workshop and design execution contributed to the team exceeding its H1 onsite adoption targets by a significant margin.

  • Conversion success, click-through rate on the optimised onboarding modal rose from 18% to 30% for targeted cohorts.
  • Business growth, these efforts supported the Shopify Meta App team in reaching a $8.2B TAM in 2024, exceeding the revised goal of $7.8B.
  • Systematic quality, I introduced a “Usability & Craft” bucket in our internal task management tool to track and resolve quality issues, and became the design contact for future feature intakes to keep the quality bar high.

Reflection

Time zones, scope and misalignment between teams could have caused a major delay. Recognising that early, escalating it and stepping up to lead the London workshop was the right call. It turned the chaos and “blurred lines” of an early project kick-off into a high-performing working model that delivered results.
