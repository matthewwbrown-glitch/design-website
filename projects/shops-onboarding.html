Meta · Shopify Meta App · 2024

Streamlining Shops onboarding and global stakeholder alignment

As lead product designer for the Shopify Meta App, I led the strategy and design for Shops onboarding in Ads Manager, aligning cross-functional teams across global offices to deliver a unified seller experience.

Overview

Before Shopify sellers could run Shops ads, they needed a shop with onsite checkout on Facebook or Instagram. The onboarding to get there was spread across multiple surfaces, and only 18% of targeted sellers clicked through, which limited how many sellers Shops ads could reach.

The Challenge

Following a recent reorganisation, the project faced significant risks due to team misalignment and unclear ownership. Working across time zones meant frequent late-night and early-morning calls, making it difficult to resolve competing priorities and reach alignment over Zoom.

  • Team misalignment: Different pillars had conflicting priorities for onboarding the “Orange” (medium-friction) and “Green” (low-friction) seller cohorts.
  • High ambiguity: I balanced the design needs of several high-impact projects while adapting to new working conditions and reduced content design support.
  • Technical complexity: The solution required updating user flows across nine surfaces and three outreach mechanisms (email, AYMT and Shopify Resource Feedback).

Strategic Approach: The London Design Sprint

To address the growing misalignment, I planned and facilitated a two-day in-person design workshop in London, bringing cross-functional partners from across the globe together to align on and finalise the product experience.

1. Facilitating stakeholder alignment

  • The room where it happened: I led the strategy and design sprint for the “Orange” cohort, while supporting the Shops Engagement product designers on the “Green” cohort.
  • Visualising the end-to-end journey: Using whiteboards, FigJam and prototypes, I mapped the end-to-end onboarding experience for both cohorts, so every cross-functional partner, from engineering to product, was aligned.
  • Establishing a working model: With alignment established, I put a working model in place that restored team momentum and enabled us to deliver designs for milestones 1 and 2 on time.

2. Design and execution

Following the workshop, I translated what we had aligned on in London into a solution the team could build from. I documented the agreed direction and outstanding questions, bringing everything together in a high-fidelity Figma prototype built from atomic components that could scale across all surfaces.

Both teams continued collaborating asynchronously on the finer details, but the core experience was locked in. The quick turnaround maintained the momentum from London and unblocked engineering to begin development.

Results & Impact

The London workshop and subsequent design execution helped the team exceed its H1 onsite adoption targets by a significant margin.

  • Conversion success: Click-through rates on the optimised onboarding modal increased from 18% to 30% across targeted seller cohorts.
  • Business growth: The work supported the Shopify Meta App team in reaching a $8.2B TAM in 2024, exceeding the revised target of $7.8B.
  • Systematic quality: I introduced a “Usability & Craft” category in our internal task management tool to track and resolve quality issues, and became the design point of contact for future feature intakes to maintain a high quality bar.

Reflection

Time zones, competing priorities and misalignment between teams could have caused significant delays. Recognising the risks early, escalating them and stepping up to lead the London workshop was the right call. It transformed the ambiguity and unclear ownership of an early project kick-off into a high-performing working model that delivered results.
